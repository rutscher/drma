What is GRVL DRMA?

🏔️

The Hilliest Possible Route

Tired of flat, windy gravel races? We found the hilliest possible route within an easy drive of the Twin Cities. 11,000 feet of climbing in 120 miles through Wisconsin's stunning Driftless region.

🆓

100% Free & Grassroots

Inspired by Minnesota's rich history of free grassroots events like Almanzo, Ragnarok 105, and Dickie Scramble. No entry fees. No corporate sponsors. Just bikes and good times.

🌲

Stunning Scenery

Cross multiple trout streams on winding roads through dense woodlands. Stop at the legendary gas station in Plum City with picnic tables and a wildlife-filled pond.

😎

No Drama Policy

The name is ironic. While gravel racing is full of drama about "unwritten rules," we're here for fun. Aero bars? Go for it. Stash pickle juice? Live your truth. Attack the feed zone? We don't judge.

FAQ

Why is it called GRVL DRMA?

Gravel racing is full of drama about "unwritten rules" - attacking in the feed zone, stashing pickle juice, using aero bars. The name is an homage to that drama, but we're actually drama-free. Also, bike racing is allergic to vowels.

Is it really free?

Yes! 100% free. No entry fees, no hidden costs. We're inspired by Minnesota's grassroots gravel events like Almanzo and Ragnarok 105.

How hard is 11,000 feet of climbing?

Very hard. The original route was 180 miles with 15,000' and it was too brutal. We scaled it back because we want to have fun out there. But make no mistake - this is an extremely hilly route.

What about support?

This is an unsupported ride. Bring everything you need. The gas station in Plum City is a great place to stop for supplies and root beer.

Can I use aero bars?

Use whatever you want. Aero bars, drop bars, mustache bars, no bars. We don't care. This is about having fun and riding bikes.

Why Bay City, Wisconsin?

It's close to the Twin Cities and has access to the incredible hills and gravel of Western Wisconsin's Driftless region. The route avoids major roads and dangerous bridges while maximizing beautiful scenery and challenging climbs.

What's the terrain like?

A mix of quiet paved and gravel roads with emphasis on gravel. Expect winding roads through dense woodlands, multiple trout stream crossings, and constant climbing. This is Laura Ingalls Wilder's stomping ground!
